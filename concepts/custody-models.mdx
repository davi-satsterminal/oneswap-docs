---
title: "Custody models"
description: "Custodial vs. self-custody — pick the right integration."
---

OneSwap v2 supports two custody models through **one** SDK. Both settle as
[atomic DvP](/concepts/atomic-dvp); they differ only in who holds the signing key.

## Custodial

The platform provisions a wallet for each user and holds its key (encrypted at
rest). The user authenticates with an account session; OneSwap signs on their
behalf.

- **Pros:** fastest to embed, no wallet UX, one-line swaps.
- **Cons:** the platform custodies funds; users trust the deployment.
- **Use when:** you're building an app that onboards users directly and wants the
  simplest possible integration.

```ts
await dex.login('alice', 'pw');
await dex.swap(1.0, true);
```

## Self-custody (interactive)

The user keeps their own key, on **any** validator. OneSwap prepares the atomic
DvP; the user's wallet signs its own leg; the operator co-signs; the ledger
settles.

- **Pros:** non-custodial — funds never leave the user's control before
  settlement; works across validators.
- **Cons:** the integrating wallet must be able to sign a transaction hash.
- **Use when:** you're a **wallet** integrating OneSwap, or serving power users who
  self-custody.

```ts
await dex.interactive.swap({ partyA, partyB, legs, signer });
```

## Choosing

| Question | Custodial | Self-custody |
|---|---|---|
| Do your users have their own Canton key? | no | yes |
| Are their parties on your validator? | yes | any validator |
| Do you want zero wallet UX? | yes | no |
| Must the user retain custody? | no | yes |

You can offer **both** in the same product — the SDK is one class.
